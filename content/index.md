# Neural Codes, Noise, and Memory: A Guide to Three Papers

These three neuroscience papers study how groups of nerve cells represent information used in memory, eye control, and navigation. The guide connects mathematical models of stored values with eye-movement recordings from monkeys and recordings of spatial activity in rodents and bats.

Begin with the reading path for paper copies, assumed knowledge, and the route through the mathematics. The guide assumes undergraduate calculus, basic probability, and linear algebra. Neuroscience, diffusion, information, filtering, and spatial random-field ideas are taught before the calculations that use them.

Part III extends this reading into electronic implementations of neural memory. It begins with von Neumann's comparison, separates stored weights from changing neural states, and examines selected neuromorphic computing research through 30 October 2026. The final chapter proposes tests connecting device physics to the accuracy of a remembered value.

Part IV explains the simulations, data-analysis methods, and software used by Burak and Fiete (2012), Ben-Shushan et al. (2022), and Mainali et al. (2025). Part introductions identify the source and role of each chapter result, separating central findings, supporting work, background, and guide-derived comparisons.
