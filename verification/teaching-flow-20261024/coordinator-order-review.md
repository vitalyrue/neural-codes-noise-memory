# Coordinator reading-path review

This record covers the larger order and the connections between chapter groups. It supplements the independent chapter reviews; it does not replace them. The inspected reader version is frozen-v1.json, with the closed-fold reading copies in first-pass/.

The book has three kinds of contribution: a mathematical limit for a memory model, an inference about the source of eye movement, and a statistical account of place-field geometry. The reading guide explains those differences before giving the reading routes. Moving its interpretation section next to those routes keeps the question of how to read the evidence together. Paper copies remain a separate lookup section with stable links.

The two Parts still serve different learning tasks. The first explains how a represented value can move while the activity persists, then relates that movement to how distinguishable nearby activity patterns are. The second compares model predictions with measurements and asks what additional assumptions would connect the temporal and spatial models. The Part 1 title benefits from naming the continuous-attractor model scope. A general phrase about all persistent activity would reach beyond the derived family of models.

The present main chapter order is retained. Moving the place-field chapter directly after the foundations would serve the spatial reader sooner, but would interrupt the mechanism, information and eye-evidence sequence. The reading guide already provides the shorter Chapter 1 to Chapter 5 route. Moving eye drift before information would bring measurements forward, but would either delay its information-based parameter scaling or require a backward jump. Neither alternative improves the main reading task enough to justify those costs.

At the Chapter 1 to 2 boundary, the reader already knows the difference between a value and the population pattern expressing it, what a fixed point and continuous attractor mean, and the variance convention for diffusion. Chapter 2 gives those ideas a specific spike-and-synapse model. Its title names diffusion along the attractor; its opening and later systematic-drift section supply the model limits.

Chapter 2 ends by distinguishing a dynamical quantity, D, from the statistical quantity to come. Chapter 3 starts from the problem of telling nearby rate patterns apart with a finite spike sample. The local diffusion formula and the required Poisson count variance remain visible. Hiding the factorial-moment check and the density-equation proof removes neither input to the information calculation.

Chapter 3 distinguishes storage delay from observation time and ends by separating diffusion from a restoring leak. Chapter 4 then follows a represented eye-position command through motor neurons and eye mechanics. The experimental calibration comes before its inversion and covariance inference; the proposed integrator follows the measured evidence. This avoids presenting that integrator as the uniquely recorded source.

The Chapter 4 to 5 change is deliberate: the reader moves from temporal motion to spatial tuning. Chapter 5 opens with the varied field patterns that need explaining, and explicitly says the sampled map is fixed as the animal moves. The earlier foundations and Part purpose make this a change of research question rather than an unannounced change of variable. The threshold and spatial scale are inferred before later predictions use them.

Chapter 5 closes by separating agreement with Gaussian field statistics from identifying synaptic weights. Chapter 6 takes that limit together with the memory and eye limits, then introduces a clearly labeled guide calculation linking a moving estimate with fixed tuning curves. Its assumptions and covariance result remain open, as does the short numerical example. The proposed joint test therefore follows the extra relationship it would need to measure.

Across the ten selected folds, the first-pass path retains first definitions, key equations, assumptions, the shortest useful examples and every figure. The hidden spans are checks, proofs and an alternative derivation. Independent Stage 3 review must still verify each particular closed boundary. This coordinator review does not approve the recorded incomplete impulse-response ending or the topology threshold-direction sentence; both require the scoped repair and recheck recorded in the review files.
