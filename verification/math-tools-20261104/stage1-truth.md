The chapter's equations and worked arithmetic are correct. I found one missing model condition that should be added before acceptance, and two small accuracy/source-location corrections. No new proof or redesign is needed.

**Finding 1, model scope, medium priority.** In the paragraph beginning "A second quantity describes what happens while the circuit stores the value" (line 105), the list of conditions for the information-diffusion bound gives weak fluctuations, a common time constant and Poisson generation, but omits tuned connectivity supporting a continuous family of stationary states in the noise-free dynamics. That condition is part of the paper's model (main PDF p. 6, final paragraph of Weak Fluctuations; source text lines 723-727). The formula is a local diffusion bound along that family, not a bound on the variance of any weakly fluctuating Poisson circuit. A circuit attracted to one fixed state, for example, can have bounded variance rather than the proposed free diffusion. Add a short plain explanation such as "for weights tuned so that a continuous range of represented values is stationary without noise." The existing zero mean drift and approximately constant coefficients condition correctly qualifies the variance-growth formula. The later caution that this is a model result is good, but does not itself identify the missing condition.

**Finding 2, source locations, low priority.** The tools-fi footnote points to SI IV at PDF pp. 10-11, but IV begins on actual PDF p. 9 and continues on p. 10. The tools-bound footnote points to SI V at PDF pp. 11-12, but V begins on actual PDF p. 10; the scalar inequality concludes on p. 11 and the multidimensional discussion continues on p. 12. Correct these to pp. 9-10 and pp. 10-12, with the initial page links 9 and 10. I checked the actual 20-page PDF with pypdf as well as the extracted source page markers. The main-paper equation and Methods page links are correct.

**Finding 3, rate definition, low priority.** The opening weights paragraph (line 9) says "A rate describes the distribution of event times." A mean firing rate by itself does not specify a distribution of event times: periodic and Poisson events can have the same mean rate. The formal definition immediately before it is correct. Prefer "A rate describes expected event frequency" or similar. The subsequent Poisson assumption supplies the extra distribution rule where it is actually needed.

The synaptic and connection account is otherwise correct. The original Eq. 7 makes each spike add one activation unit and gives exponential decay between spikes. The original Eq. 8 uses row i for receiver and column j for sender. The units assigned to activation, input, bias and weight are consistent. Positive and negative weights affect firing in the stated direction when the transfer function increases. A one-unit jump changes the receiving input by W_ij. Fixed weights, decaying activation and later spikes form the described feedback. With W=0, all rates depend only on the biases, and the contribution from initial activation decays; this statement does not incorrectly claim that later random fluctuations disappear.

The two-unit example correctly substitutes W_12=W_21=-1 and phi(g)=g/tau into the deterministic rate equations. It gives tau times each derivative equal to b-s_1-s_2, so the total relaxes with rate 2/tau and the difference is constant. Both (12,8) and (8,12) have total b=20 and differences 4 and -4. At a fixed point the inputs equal s_1 and s_2, so both rates are positive exactly for absolute difference less than b. The calculation is clearly labeled as deterministic and reconstructed for teaching; it does not claim a noiseless realization of the spiking model. The paper's two-neuron model permits arbitrary positive b for this calculation, even though its simulation used b=5000.

The FI calculation correctly separates finite-observation information I_h from information rate J. For independent constant-rate Poisson counts with theta held fixed, mean and variance h times rate lead to exact FI h times slope squared divided by rate. These contributions add only with the stated conditional independence. In an evolving recurrent circuit, using these stationary rates during a brief window is an approximation, which the chapter states. The definition is local and correctly avoids treating FI as stored bits or as a guarantee of distinguishing distant duplicate patterns. The positive-rate condition avoids division by zero. The bar is correctly identified with the noise-free steady tuning curve, not a claim that noisy trajectories remain at a fixed point.

The FI example's means, variances, slopes and arithmetic are correct: the respective count slopes 0.2 and 1 give information 0.02 and 0.5 per degree squared, a factor of 25; the independent sum is 0.52. The degree and second units assigned to I_h, J and D are correct. The explanation of the denominator correctly avoids claiming that scaling an entire tuning curve upward necessarily reduces FI.

The Cramer Rao statement is valid for regular locally unbiased estimators, with local unbiasedness correctly including agreement of the mean to first order. It does not claim every finite sample reaches the bound, and the linked later chapter states the smoothness, differentiation and positive finite information conditions. The finite I_h bound is not improperly used as the proof of the dynamical inequality. The separate physical self-estimation explanation is correctly called a motivation, with the dynamical proof deferred.
