## Shared assumptions and symbols

The physical state is synaptic activation, not a noiseless vector of firing rates. Attractor coordinate $\theta$ labels the fixed point reached by noise-free return flow; $\Theta$ is its initial value. A bar means a steady value. A prime means derivative with respect to $\theta$. Each spike adds one unit to $s_i$. All synaptic time constants equal $\tau$ in the basic model. The network is tuned to a smooth continuous family of fixed points, with a neutral tangent and stable other modes. The diffusion calculation is leading order in weak input fluctuations. Newly generated Poisson fluctuations are independent conditional on the current state. Full spike trains can become correlated through recurrence. The convention is $\operatorname{Var}(\Delta\theta)=2D\Delta t$, so $D$ is half the displacement variance rate.

The readout section makes additional assumptions: constant local $D,J>0$, an unbounded coordinate with no systematic drift, no informative prior, locally unbiased estimates, and a Gaussian observation model that separates state-motion noise from count-observation noise. The formulas are exact in that Gaussian model. They do not establish exact inference from the full recurrent jump process when the same spikes cause state motion and supply observations.

## Foundations to link, rather than label as new major 2012 results

- Model: $ds_i=-s_i\,dt/\tau+dN_i$, $g_i=\sum_jW_{ij}s_j+b_i$, $r_i=\phi(g_i)$; $\bar s_i=\tau\bar r_i$. Main Eqs. 1 and 7-9. [Spike, activation and rate model](ch-foundations.html#sec-memory-spikes).
- Constant-rate Poisson counts: $P(K=k)=e^{-rh}(rh)^k/k!$, $E[K]=\operatorname{Var}K=rh$. [Poisson derivation](ch-foundations.html#sec-foundations-poisson).
- Brownian accumulation: $\theta(t)=\Theta+\sqrt{2D}W(t)$; $\operatorname{Cov}(\theta(s)-\Theta,\theta(t)-\Theta)=2D\min(s,t)$. With a constant velocity $v$, MSD is $v^2t^2+2Dt$ while variance remains $2Dt$. [Foundation derivations](ch-persistence.html#sec-foundations-diffusion).
- Two-rate example $r_1=b+k\theta$, $r_2=b-k\theta$, $\dot x=-ax$, $\dot\theta=0$ is a teaching example showing stable pattern shape alongside a wrong value. It is not a separately fitted source result. [Example](ch-persistence.html#sec-foundations-attractor-example).

## Printed-source issues to retain explicitly

1. Main Eq. 2 lacks $(g_i')^2$ in the numerator. SI S18 has the square, and direct variance projection requires it. The guide cites a 2017 author correction at DOI `10.1073/pnas.1706051114`. That external correction was not independently refreshed in this local inventory.
2. SI S40 prints $D=\theta/(2\tau)$ for mutual excitation. With $(u_1,u_2)=(1,1)$ and $(v_1,v_2)=(1/2,1/2)$, direct projection gives $2D=\theta/(2\tau)$, hence $D=\theta/(4\tau)$. This agrees with S41's variance $\Theta t/(2\tau)$ under the source's $2Dt$ convention. Preserve as a local factor-of-two check, not an author correction.
3. SI S71 prints $f+\epsilon f''=0$, but its hyperbolic-cosine S73 solves $f-\epsilon f''=0$. The guide's independent precision recursion confirms S75. S74 also prints an upper integration limit $T$ while the observation duration in the formula is $\Delta T$.
4. SI S77 displays a sum of squares when the variance of the average needs the square of the sum, including off-diagonal time covariances. S78 and the correct S79 use those covariances. Use the double-integral derivation in the table.
5. Main Eq. 6 and SI S83 have the lower-bound issue explained above. Do not turn $\sqrt{2D/J}\le2D\tau$ into the opposite inequality.
6. SI S85 prints a positive rate-decay block; differentiating S84 gives $-I/\tau_m$. The guide uses the negative block.
7. SI's exponential-necessity claim needs extra restrictions across cells and states. The guide's zero-bias mutual-excitation family with linear $\phi(g)=g/\tau$ has $2D=\theta/(2\tau)$, $J=2/(\tau\theta)$, hence equality at every positive state. Use the general pointwise equality condition and state exponential transfer as sufficient.
