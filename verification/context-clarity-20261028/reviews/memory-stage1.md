## Recommended repairs

### M1. Identify whose synaptic activation is tracked

- Passage: `02-memory-dynamics.md`, section `MEMORY-SPIKES`, lines 14-16: “Incoming activations determine new firing rates; the resulting spikes renew those activations.” / “The activation $s_i$ rises by one at each of its spikes and otherwise decays”. The later equation uses $g_i=\sum_j W_{ij}s_j+b_i$ (lines 40-47).
- Missing context: the equation defines the mechanics, but the biological referent of $s_i$ remains loose. It is the decaying effect generated by spikes from neuron $i$, which contributes to the inputs of the neurons receiving its connections. It is not neuron $i$'s own received total input $g_i$, nor its firing rate $r_i$. A newcomer can otherwise assign the activation to the wrong side of the connection.
- Proposed addition before the $s_i$ equation: “The model uses $s_i$ to track the decaying synaptic effect left by neuron $i$'s outgoing spikes. A receiving neuron $j$ gets a contribution $W_{ji}s_i$ from those spikes.” Keep the one-unit convention and subsequent equations unchanged. Alternatively give the same explanation immediately before the $g_i$ equation using the existing $j$ to $i$ notation.
- Needed support: verified in `burak-fiete-2012-main.txt`, lines 104-106 and Methods lines 684-703, Eqs. 7-9. This is the source's model, not a direct measurement of synapses.
- Later explanation: lines 47 and 196 partly supply the relationship through weights and projected noise. They do not explicitly separate outgoing activation from received total input. The figure caption also describes the trace correctly but does not locate it in the connection pathway. This is a short missing bridge, not a need for more cellular detail.

### M2. Define neural integration at its first dependent use

- Passage: `01-foundations.md`, `FOUNDATIONS-COVARIANCE`, line 128: “This identity will distinguish random memory diffusion from a biased or leaky integrator.” Connected later passage: `03b-memory-readout.md`, `MEMORY-FIG3-AND-CAUTION`, line 307: “The short readout interval is not a short lifetime of the underlying integrator. A tuned integrator can continue to represent the mean of accumulated inputs while its variance grows.”
- Missing context: the reader has learned a stored value and diffusion, but has not learned what a neural integrator accumulates or what “leaky” means. Knowing calculus alone does not identify a brain circuit's input and represented output.
- Proposed repair: in foundations, replace the second sentence with “This identity will help distinguish random spreading of a stored value from a systematic change in its average.” At the later integration discussion, insert: “A neural integrator is a circuit whose represented value accumulates an incoming signal over time. An ideal integrator retains the accumulated value when that signal stops; a leaky integrator gradually loses it toward a baseline.” Then retain the existing explanation of mean versus variance, with its qualifiers. This keeps the basic foundations focused while teaching the term when the argument actually needs it.
- Needed support: `burak-fiete-2012-si.txt`, lines 930-938, “Integration time constant”; `burak-fiete-2012-main.txt`, Discussion lines 617-627. Those passages directly support accumulated input, growing variance and the distinction from return toward a single fixed point.
- Later explanation: chapter 6 line 307 explains leak and the maintained mean, but begins by presuming the integrator's role; it is too late for chapter 1's first use. No adequate earlier definition in the reading guide or assigned chapters.

### M3. Explain the new rate relaxation step in ordinary terms

- Passage: `03b-memory-readout.md`, `MEMORY-EXTENSIONS`, lines 220-227: “If those dynamics contain both synaptic and rate relaxation, which time constant matters?” / “The supplement adds rate relaxation with time constant $\tau_m$”.
- Missing context: the basic model obtains $r_i$ immediately from $\phi(g_i)$, whereas the extension makes the rate approach that value gradually. The added equation states this accurately, but the reader is not told that this is the second physical/model stage or how it differs from synaptic decay. This is the connection on which adding the two time constants rests.
- Proposed replacement of line 222: “In the basic model, the firing rate changes immediately when the summed input changes. The extension instead lets $r_i$ approach $\phi(g_i)$ gradually, with time constant $\tau_m$, while synaptic activation still decays with time constant $\tau$.” Retain the equations and noise placement.
- Needed support: `burak-fiete-2012-si.txt`, lines 940-951, “A model with two time scales”, Eq. S84. The source explicitly contrasts the instantaneous rule with averaging after the nonlinearity. Calling this “rate relaxation” is fine once that change is explained.
- Later explanation: the matrix and left-vector calculation trace its consequence, and line 254 properly limits the result to this model. Neither supplies this missing plain-language comparison. No need to teach membrane biophysics for the present derivation.

### M4. Distinguish symmetric connection weights from symmetry across represented positions

- Passage: `02-memory-dynamics.md`, `MEMORY-SYMMETRIC`, line 254: “Translation symmetry is unnecessary.” Related passage: `02b-memory-long-delays.md`, `MEMORY-FIGURES`, line 126: “This second design matters because the projection argument does not require rotational symmetry.”
- Missing context: $W_{ij}=W_{ji}$ has been defined, but translation/rotation symmetry has not. These are different properties. A newcomer may understand the sentence as contradicting the section's symmetric-weight requirement.
- Proposed repair of the first occurrence: “The derivation requires equal weights in the two directions between each pair of cells. It does not require the network to have the same form after shifting the represented coordinate, a property called translation symmetry.” At the ring discussion, a short reminder can say that rotational symmetry is the corresponding invariance under an angular shift. Keep the claim that the general projection argument has still broader scope.
- Needed support: main text's symmetric-weight result and the contrast between rotationally symmetric ring and mutual-inhibition designs (`burak-fiete-2012-main.txt`, lines 174-217); SI S12-S18. The distinction also follows directly from the guide's formulas and examples.
- Later explanation: `02b` line 119 gives a ring with weights determined by differences of preferred angles, which illustrates the symmetry but does not say what “symmetry” means. The later sentence therefore does not fully repair the first use.

## Optional short clarifications

### M5. Shared “drive” can be stated as shared input

- Passage: `01-foundations.md`, `FOUNDATIONS-POISSON`, line 72: “If cells share an additional fluctuating drive, that common variation will not disappear in the same way.”
- Current strong reading: the next words “that common variation” and the preceding independence comparison make the mathematical point recoverable. This is not a confirmed false claim.
- Optional repair: “If a common varying input makes several cells' firing rates rise and fall together, that shared variation will not disappear in the same way.” This identifies what acts on what without introducing a new term.
- Needed support: no new empirical claim; it is the shared-variation qualification already supported by the surrounding covariance and conditional-independence teaching. Do not add an asserted biological source without separate evidence.
- Later explanation: the following conditional-state paragraph and later covariance section explain the statistical distinction. This optional edit would make the first biological referent clearer, not add a missing mathematical derivation.

### M6. Make the receiving readout circuit explicit once

- Passage: `03-memory-information.md`, opening line 3: “A downstream neuron reads a memory through spikes.”
- Current strong reading: “through spikes” fits the established synaptic pathway, and later text makes the observer mathematical. Do not claim this sentence falsely promises that one cell performs optimal estimation.
- Optional repair: “A neuron receiving spikes from the memory circuit can obtain information about its stored value from those spikes.” The next sentence already introduces collecting spikes, and the readout chapter can retain “observer” for the mathematical estimator.
- Needed support: verified main Discussion, `burak-fiete-2012-main.txt` lines 587-591, and SI Section VI, lines 649-658. Those passages distinguish a recipient of spikes from an ideal observer used to assess possible accuracy.
- Later explanation: `03b-memory-readout.md` line 195 explains that a downstream area receives some of the memory circuit's spikes. This is enough to support the intended meaning but appears after the first use. An added definition at every later use would be unnecessary.

## Retain decisions and limits

- `01-foundations`: rate is explicitly expected spike count per time; the neuron index is distinguished from physical location; place/grid cells and the relevant brain areas have enough context for their introductory role. The Poisson example distinguishes counts, rates and correlated variation. Captions identify constructed examples and keep their conditions.
- `01b-persistence`: current activities define state, and the two-rate trace explains neutral versus restored directions and why coherent firing can store the wrong angle. Diffusion is taught as an across-trial spread, not motion away from zero on every individual path. Fixed spatial randomness is distinguished from changing memory. No recommended context repair.
- `02-memory-dynamics`: the feedback cycle, noise approximation, projection geometry and per-cell effect are explicitly taught. The remaining issue is locating $s_i$ biologically (M1) and explaining the extra symmetry claim (M4). The examples and captions are explicitly constructions rather than recordings.
- `02b-memory-long-delays`: the repeated-trial density, angular wrapping, deterministic versus noise-induced mean movement, and finite-spike readout uncertainty have suitable context. Simulation-versus-animal evidence is explicitly distinguished. M4 is the only connected terminology gap identified.
- `03-memory-information`: information is taught as local distinguishability through rates and random counts. Its internal coordinate is separated from an external stimulus; high firing alone is not claimed to guarantee useful information. Controlled comparisons state what remains fixed. No additional biological setting is needed beyond optional M6.
- `03b-memory-readout`: the observer's task and available spikes are stated; the observation $y_k$ is correctly described as a summary, not a new physical measurement. Unequal spike access and its assumptions are explicit. M2 and M3 supply two missing links without adding a separate neuroscience survey.
