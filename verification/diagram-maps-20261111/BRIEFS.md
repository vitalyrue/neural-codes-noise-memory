# Draft map briefs for the neuroscience guide

These are planning briefs, not approved briefs or image reviews. Their listed sections are starting points, not a complete inventory of teaching points. Before generation, inspect each source including examples and equations, freeze its hash, record all distinct points and source loci, select visible points, and check that every chapter opening map shows strictly more than 60% of its complete inventory. Plan each Part separately. Review the finished image for meaning, readability and coverage. Existing figures elsewhere in a chapter cannot supply map coverage. Captions and accessible descriptions belong outside artwork. Do not imply guide examples are experimental data or that correlations establish mechanisms.

## Part maps

- **Part I** — `plan/chapter-plan.yaml`, `part:MECHANISMS`, Chapters 1–6. Recurrent neural feedback maintains an activity pattern; spike noise shifts the represented angle; Fisher information constrains diffusion under stated assumptions; decoding a moving value creates a readout tradeoff. Show the distinction between stable activity and stable value, and between network storage and observer readout.
- **Part II** — `plan/chapter-plan.yaml`, `part:OBSERVATIONS`, Chapters 7–13. Show two separate branches: shared neural drive through motor neurons and the eye plant to drift measurements; fixed spatial input through threshold to place fields and their statistics. Bring the branches together only as a comparison of questions and evidence, not one proven circuit.
- **Part III** — `plan/chapter-plan.yaml`, `part:HARDWARE`, Chapters 14–18. Distinguish stored connection settings, dynamic state, and represented value; compare feedback architectures, discrete restoration and continuous drift; show device choices and matched trials/readout as tests. Do not claim all listed circuits are neural networks.
- **Part IV** — `plan/chapter-plan.yaml`, `part:SIMULATIONS`, Chapters 19–22. Compare time-evolving recurrent spike simulation, neural command through eye mechanics, and thresholded spatial maps, then connect each to observables, data and numerical/reproducibility checks. Keep fitted summaries distinct from additional comparisons.

## Part I chapters

- **1 Foundations** — `01-foundations.md`: `sec:FOUNDATIONS-EYE-ACTION`, `sec:MEMORY-SPIKES`, `sec:FOUNDATIONS-SPIKES`, `sec:FOUNDATIONS-POISSON`, `sec:FOUNDATIONS-SIMULATION`, `sec:FOUNDATIONS-COVARIANCE`. Spikes renew synaptic activation, weighted inputs set rates, population activity can encode eye angle, rate variability matters; simulation tracks a noisy stored value and covariance distinguishes shared fluctuations.
- **2 Persistence** — `01b-persistence.md`: `sec:FOUNDATIONS-ATTRACTOR`, `sec:PERSISTENCE-EYE-MECHANICS`, `sec:FOUNDATIONS-DIFFUSION`, `sec:FOUNDATIONS-RANDOMNESS`. Contrast restoring motion toward a state family with persistent displacements along it, and eye restoring forces with neural muscle drive; distinguish temporal random motion from a fixed random spatial map.
- **3 Memory dynamics** — `02-memory-dynamics.md`: `sec:MEMORY-WEAK-NOISE`, `sec:MEMORY-LINEARIZATION`, `sec:MEMORY-DIFFUSION`, `sec:MEMORY-SYMMETRIC`. Spike fluctuations enter a recurrent state; stable disturbances decay while the projected neutral component changes memory; symmetric connections permit a tuning-curve expression. Avoid suggesting left and right directions coincide in general.
- **4 Long delays** — `02b-memory-long-delays.md`: `sec:MEMORY-FIGURES`, `sec:MEMORY-LONG-TIME`, `sec:MEMORY-DRIFT-SCOPE`. Compare short-time diffusion with wraparound or boundaries at long times; distinguish stored-state motion from readout error and random spreading from systematic drift.
- **5 Information and diffusion** — `03-memory-information.md`: `sec:MEMORY-FI`, `sec:MEMORY-BOUND`, `sec:MEMORY-EXTENSIONS`, `sec:MEMORY-SATURATION`, `sec:MEMORY-COMPARISONS`. Tuning slopes and spike variability give local Fisher information; under the paper’s model assumptions information bounds diffusion; distinguish a bound from equality and compare changed parameters only with explicit fixed quantities.
- **6 Readout** — `03b-memory-readout.md`: `sec:MEMORY-READOUT-SETUP`, `sec:MEMORY-IDEAL`, `sec:MEMORY-NAIVE`, `sec:MEMORY-PARTIAL`, `sec:MEMORY-FIG3-AND-CAUTION`, `sec:MEMORY-MINIMUM-VARIANCE`. The state moves during observation; accumulating spikes reduces sampling noise but earlier spikes describe an older state. Contrast ideal weighting with equal weighting and limited access; preserve the printed-bound qualification.

## Part II chapters

- **7 Eye drift** — `04-eye-drift.md`: `sec:EYE-PATHWAY`, `sec:EYE-RECORDINGS`, `sec:EYE-FORWARD-FIT`, `sec:EYE-INVERSE-FILTER`, `sec:EYE-SHARED-VARIANCE`, `sec:EYE-CHI`. A shared upstream command acts through individual motor neurons and the eye plant; paired recordings and calibrated forward/inverse filtering estimate a common movement component under calibration and independence assumptions.
- **8 Eye timing** — `04b-eye-timing.md`: `sec:EYE-MEASUREMENT-NOISE`, `sec:EYE-FILTERED-MSD`. Measurement error offsets displacement variance; mechanical filtering shapes temporal growth, with a bounded filtered-white-noise curve and continued growth for filtered diffusion. Specify what is measured against lag.
- **9 Eye circuit** — `04c-eye-circuit.md`: `sec:EYE-CIRCUIT-MODEL`, `sec:EYE-SCALING`, `sec:EYE-FIGURES`, `sec:EYE-LIMITS`. Noisy neural integration, motor units, muscle/plant, and feedback generate modeled motion. Show how spike variability and parameters change the scale and what data constrain; an agreement does not uniquely identify the biological circuit.
- **10 Place fields** — `05-place-fields.md`: `sec:PLACE-GP`, `sec:PLACE-KAC-RICE`, `sec:PLACE-INFERENCE`. A smooth spatial input threshold creates active fields and gaps; upcrossings plus boundary correction count fields; occupied fraction and lengths help distinguish threshold from spatial scale.
- **11 Place sizes** — `05b-place-sizes.md`: `sec:PLACE-RARE-PEAKS`, `sec:PLACE-SIZE-LAW`, `sec:PLACE-SLICES`, `sec:PLACE-SLOPES`. A high peak locally approximates a parabola; threshold intersection produces dimensional size laws, while slices and boundary slopes give separate checks. Mark the high-threshold regime explicitly.
- **12 Place tests** — `05c-place-tests.md`: `sec:PLACE-TOPOLOGY`, `sec:PLACE-DATA`, `sec:PLACE-WIRING`. Compare equal-count maps with different holes, Euler characteristic and boundary contributions; separate fitted parameters from tests; random weighted input is one possible source of Gaussian spatial structure, not identified biological wiring.
- **13 Synthesis** — `06-synthesis.md`: `sec:SYNTHESIS-QUESTIONS`, `sec:SYNTHESIS-JOINT-CALCULATION`, `sec:SYNTHESIS-EVIDENCE`, `sec:SYNTHESIS-RELATED`, `sec:SYNTHESIS-NEXT`. Separate temporal memory dynamics, motor output, and static spatial maps; show a shared position shift changing cells with different tuning slopes and a proposed joint test, not a result jointly established by the papers.

## Part III chapters

- **14 Hardware memory** — `07-hardware-memory.md`: `sec:HARDWARE-MEMORY-ROLES`, `sec:HARDWARE-MEMORY-STORAGE`, `sec:HARDWARE-MEMORY-UPDATES`. Distinguish stored synaptic setting, changing electrical state and represented cue; show how bits, capacitor voltages and conductances affect later updates.
- **15 Electronic architectures** — `11-electronic-architectures.md`: `sec:ELECTRONIC-OVERVIEW`, `sec:ELECTRONIC-FLIPFLOP`, `sec:ELECTRONIC-MULTILEVEL`, `sec:ELECTRONIC-RING`, `sec:ELECTRONIC-OSCILLATOR`, `sec:ELECTRONIC-SPIKES`, `sec:ELECTRONIC-READOUT`. Compare feedback and stored variable in a bistable flip-flop, multistable device, recurrent activity ring and oscillator; connect pulse/analog variables and distinguish drift from readout information.
- **16 Hardware dynamics** — `08-hardware-dynamics.md`: `sec:HARDWARE-DYNAMICS-FEEDBACK`, `sec:HARDWARE-DYNAMICS-ERRORS`, `sec:HARDWARE-DYNAMICS-EVIDENCE`. Feedback sustains a state; discrete attractors restore small perturbations whereas neutral directions preserve displacement and accumulate noise. Identify silicon examples as demonstrations of their own models.
- **17 Neuromorphic systems** — `09-neuromorphic-systems.md`: `sec:NEUROMORPHIC-SYSTEMS-ARCHITECTURES`, `sec:NEUROMORPHIC-SYSTEMS-ADVANCES`, `sec:NEUROMORPHIC-SYSTEMS-LIMITS`. Compare digital and analog placement of computation and physical state; distinguish temporal computation, adaptation and device persistence as separate reported achievements with costs.
- **18 Hardware tests** — `10-hardware-tests.md`: `sec:HARDWARE-TESTS-PROTOCOL`, `sec:HARDWARE-TESTS-PARAMETERS`, `sec:HARDWARE-TESTS-COMPARISON`. A cue, repeated delays, state observation and output measurement separate mean drift from spread; component decay, bias and noise interact through network dynamics; fair comparisons require matched time and readout.

## Part IV chapters

- **19 Memory simulations** — `11-simulating-memory.md`: `sec:SIMULATING-MEMORY-STATE`, `sec:SIMULATING-MEMORY-RING`, `sec:SIMULATING-MEMORY-CONTROLS`, `sec:SIMULATING-MEMORY-OUTPUTS`. Generate spikes, update recurrent synapses, decode trajectories and compare spreading with theory; ring boundaries and alternative network designs test scope. Distinguish an analytical curve from simulated network data.
- **20 Eye simulations** — `12-simulating-eye-drift.md`: `sec:SIMULATING-EYE-DATA`, `sec:SIMULATING-EYE-PATH`, `sec:SIMULATING-EYE-CONTROLS`, `sec:SIMULATING-EYE-TOOLS`. Measured spikes and movements constrain model comparisons; simulated integrator, motor neurons, plant and delayed feedback generate movement, with stage-specific controls. Separate code from recorded data.
- **21 Place simulations** — `13-simulating-place-fields.md`: `sec:SIMULATING-PLACE-GENERATE`, `sec:SIMULATING-PLACE-FIT`, `sec:SIMULATING-PLACE-MEASURE`, `sec:SIMULATING-PLACE-TOOLS`. Generate a smooth random map, threshold and detect fields, compare matched fitted summaries and independent checks; show sampling and processing as changes to measured fields.
- **22 Simulation tools** — `14-simulation-tools.md`: `sec:SIMULATION-TOOLS-ROUTES`, `sec:SIMULATION-TOOLS-SOFTWARE`, `sec:SIMULATION-TOOLS-RESOLUTION`, `sec:SIMULATION-TOOLS-RECORD`. Match original code or teaching reconstruction to the question; select tools for operations, verify spatial/time resolution and save parameters, seeds and measurement decisions.
