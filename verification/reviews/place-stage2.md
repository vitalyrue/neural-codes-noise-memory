## Findings

**PF-S2-01: remove unused normalized-covariance notation, PLACE-GP lines 24–33.** The line $R(\Delta x)=C(\Delta x)/C(0)$ introduces a second covariance symbol that never appears again in the chapter. Deletion test: remove that line from the display and read directly from $z=h/\sigma_h$ to $F=a[z-q]_+$, then into the following variable definitions. No later step needs $R$; all derivative, scale, conditioning and slice calculations use $C$ or $z$. Keep $z$, $q$, $a$, the positive-part notation and the paper-to-guide symbol conversion. Delete only the $R$ definition and its separator. This reduces an actual symbol-tracking burden without dropping any calculation.

**PF-S2-02: explain the two meanings of “nondegenerate,” PLACE-GP line 22, PLACE-KAC-RICE line 99, PLACE-RARE-PEAKS line 160 and PLACE-TOPOLOGY lines 316–318.** No earlier chapter supplies these meanings. “Nondegenerate joint distributions,” “under nondegeneracy,” “nondegenerate derivatives,” and “nondegenerate critical points” currently ask the reader to recognize a theorem condition without saying what could go wrong. Deletion test: deleting the conditions would wrongly broaden the imported mathematical results, so preserve their substance. At the Gaussian-distribution use, say that the relevant height/derivative vector has an invertible covariance matrix, so it has a joint density rather than lying on an exact linear constraint. At the crossing-count use, state the needed simple-crossing condition in the existing slope language: crossings have nonzero slope, with tangencies excluded under the regularity assumptions. At the critical-point use, explain that a critical point has zero gradient and is nondegenerate when its Hessian is invertible. These are different conditions; one definition should not be reused as if it covered both. The Hessian and determinant are already introduced at lines 162 and 318, and invertibility belongs to the declared linear-algebra background. No proof of the full theorem is needed.

**PF-S2-03: use or introduce the geometric meanings where the proof needs them, PLACE-SLICES line 278 and PLACE-TOPOLOGY line 364.** Apply the deletion test separately:

- “affine plane” can be “flat plane.” This keeps the needed exclusion of curved surfaces without adding an unfamiliar geometry label. No later argument uses affine coordinates or affine transformations.
- Delete “Thresholding commutes with restriction:” and start the existing sentence “First slicing and then thresholding gives the same active portions as thresholding and then slicing.” The full operation-order meaning is already present after the colon. Removing the extra label loses no result.
- “with no periodic identification” means opposite faces are not joined. Keep that condition because it changes the boundary terms, but state it that way. The phrase is not defined earlier.
- The “tangential calculation” and “outward normal gradient components” distinguish derivatives along a face from derivatives perpendicular to it, directed out of the box. Preserve those two derivative directions. Introduce their meaning at this use, for example “On each face, derivatives along the face give the same calculation as above. Each derivative perpendicular to that face and directed out of the box must have the required sign…” The original technical words may remain in parentheses if wanted for reading the source. The boundary-factor derivation depends on this distinction, so deleting the whole sentence would lose teaching.

**PF-S2-04: remove unexplained comparison-score numbers from the main explanation, PLACE-DATA line 412.** The three quantities $\Delta\mathrm{LLR}$, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}$ and $\Delta\mathrm{AIC}$ and their precise values do no work in the later argument. Deletion test: retain the conclusion that likelihood comparisons, including criteria that penalize parameter count, favor the Gaussian model over the fitted lognormal; no reader calculation or figure interpretation depends on the three numbers. A sufficient replacement for the first two sentences is: “For the long-tunnel data, the authors compared the Gaussian excursion model with a fitted lognormal using likelihoods and criteria that penalize fitted parameter count. Those comparisons favored the Gaussian model.” Keep the remaining distinction between fitting and prediction on new data, and all the simulation/error-bar limits. The exact three reported values may stay in the footnote for source inspection; they need not be in the main teaching path. This preserves the evidence comparison and avoids adding unused score names.

**PF-S2-05: explain the changed field locations directly, PLACE-DATA line 420.** “The same framework” means the model just fitted; “despite remapping” introduces a neuroscience term that no earlier chapter defines. Deletion test: the shared threshold must still explain why the two direction maps have correlated counts/sizes even though their field locations can differ. Keep that contrast, but use its meaning. Suggested local forms: “The model also allows differences between cells” and “this shared cell property creates correlations between flight directions even when field locations differ between directions.” Keep the independent-map setup, the two newly fitted distribution parameters and the additional length/gap comparison. No general remapping lesson is needed for this one use.

**PF-S2-06: simplify three related-work descriptions without adding side lessons, SYNTHESIS-RELATED lines 77, 81 and 83.** Each paragraph has a useful reason to remain: storage assumptions, alternative sources of place fields, and the possible sensory benefit of drift. The labels below are unnecessary for those comparisons and are not defined on the preceding reading path.

- Line 77, “treat persistent-activity storage as a noisy channel”: the useful point is how noise, delay and item count limit recall, and how encoding changes that prediction. A sufficient combined form is “Koyluoglu and colleagues ask how noise limits recall as the delay and number of stored items increase.” Keep the next sentence about transforming information before and after storage. The communication-channel label adds no later dependency here. If retained to identify their method, explain it as a noisy transformation from the initially stored value to the recalled value.
- Line 81, “leaves a mechanistic identification problem to be resolved with…”: keep the question of which circuit produces the maps and the listed measurements. Use “leaves open which circuit mechanism produces these maps; input, connectivity, learning or perturbation measurements could distinguish the possibilities.” The exact replacement need not use a semicolon. No precision is lost by replacing the abstract label with its question.
- Line 83, “with retinal coding and Bayesian decoding”: the paragraph only uses the result that eye motion can help fine discrimination, not the Bayesian method. A sufficient form is “Nghiem and colleagues combine human eye tracking with a model of how eye movement affects visual signals to explain why an intermediate amount of fixational motion can help fine visual discrimination.” Keep the final distinction between sensory benefit and the motion's generator. If the author chooses to retain the precise decoder name, briefly explain what evidence it combines; merely adding “Bayesian” does not teach the method. The exact paper title and the footnote's primary-method description should remain unchanged.
