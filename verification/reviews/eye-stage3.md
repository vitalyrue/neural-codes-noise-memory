### EYE-S3-01: connect the fitted differential equation to its spike response

Location: EYE-INVERSE-FILTER, paragraph p84, lines 63–89 in its connected passage. The key sentence is “Each such term corresponds to a causal exponential response ...”.

Reader task: reconstruct why a single spike produces the displayed double-exponential kernel, including its coefficient. The current text teaches the exponential-component ratio H_0(p), factorization and the two decay times. It then invokes the inverse correspondence between 1/(p+1/tau) and a causal exponential. That correspondence is a transform-theory result, not a consequence already explained to this reader. Undergraduate calculus, probability and linear algebra do not supply an assumed course in transforms or control. Neither Chapters 1–3 nor the earlier part of this section teaches this rule. The kernel formula is correct, as stage 1 established; a correct formula after the phrase “corresponds to” still leaves the reader to supply a central reasoning step.

A compact direct repair avoids another subject prerequisite. Let h_0 be the response to a unit spike. For u>0 the input is zero, so it obeys the homogeneous equation. Substitute the two exponentials already obtained from factorization and write

`h_0(u)=A exp(-u/tau_+) + B exp(-u/tau_-)`.

Explain the initial conditions: position is continuous across the impulse, so h_0(0+)=0, and integrating the differential equation across the spike gives m h_0'(0+)=1. The position and velocity before the causal response are zero. Thus A+B=0 and `A(1/tau_- - 1/tau_+)=1/m`, giving the coefficient `tau_+ tau_- / [m(tau_+-tau_-)]`. This uses differentiation, integration over a unit impulse, and the already taught meaning of causality. Alternatively, teach an equivalent first-order impulse-response rule and show the partial-fraction coefficients explicitly. No full transform course is needed.

This missing explanation matters to the guide's goal that the central calculations be reconstructible. It is not a request to rederive every algebraic simplification. The existing normalization and numerical example should remain after the repaired derivation. Any new jump-condition wording should receive a focused truth check as well as an understanding recheck.
