### EYE-S1-01: show the condition that makes this network an integrator

Passage: lines 362–372, especially “The weights were optimized to make an approximate continuum of steady states” and “In the absence of noise, a stored position can persist.” Source: P2 p. 11, Eqs. 23–26 and the weight-optimization paragraph. Rule: math contract, derivation/mechanism; pedagogy contract lines 19–27 and 338–357. Severity: medium, missing intermediate reasoning for the requested step-by-step guide.

The equations specify a nonlinear recurrent network. Recurrence alone does not explain persistence. The preceding chapters teach that arbitrary recurrent weights generally produce isolated fixed points: `02-memory-dynamics.md` lines 62–71. They do not supply the deterministic condition for this different saturating network. The target calls the weight search an optimization but does not say what relation it must make hold. Consequently the reader has to reconstruct the central connection between these equations and an approximately neutral memory direction.

Repair: replace spikes by their mean rates, set F=0, and define q_i^R(E)=lambda_i^R(E)/(lambda_t+lambda_i^R(E)), with the corresponding left expression. Multiplying the synaptic equations by the readout weights gives

`tau_s * dE_OI/dt = -E_OI + G(E_OI)`, where `G(E)=sum_i eta_i[q_i^R(E)-q_i^L(E)]`.

An exact continuum would require G(E)=E throughout the represented range. Its local neutral condition is G'(E)=1, because a small displacement obeys `tau_s * d(delta E)/dt = [G'(E)-1] delta E`. The optimized finite network only approximates these conditions. State that small residual mismatch can produce deterministic drift and that persistence is approximate over the modeled range and time. This is a teaching reconstruction directly from the printed equations, not a claim that the source prints these extra equations or provides an exact continuum.

Strongest reasonable reading considered: “can persist” need not promise perfect indefinite storage, and the word “approximate” already helps. Thus the finding is principally the missing condition/derivation, not an accusation that the source's optimization claim is false.

### EYE-S1-02: distinguish a short MSD lag from response latency

Passage: line 404, “Its delay makes it a poor explanation for changes occurring before a new visual signal could return.” Connected equations: lines 398–402. Source: P2 Eq. 34; P2SI pp. 4–5 and Extended Data Fig. 3, p. 9. Severity: medium, the reason supplied does not by itself establish the conclusion for lag statistics.

The preceding derivation concerns the MSD at a time separation inside an ongoing process. A short separation is not the same thing as time elapsed since a newly imposed perturbation. Also, this model's F=A[E(t-tau_d)-E_OI(t)] contains the immediate term -A E_OI(t). With a zero delayed history and a newly displaced internal value, F is already nonzero before a new eye-position measurement returns. In a running feedback loop, past measurements can affect present short-interval statistics. Delay therefore does not alone establish a lower lag below which feedback has little effect.

Repair: tie the particular result to the actual with/without-feedback comparison: Extended Data Fig. 3b–c shows the model's larger effect on the slope at longer lags, while its amplitude changes at all lags. Retain the biological delay as context for responses to new visual changes, and state that it is not a hard cutoff for effects on an ongoing MSD. The text already says the division concerns this model, and does not explicitly claim a hard cutoff; preserve those qualifications. The supported conclusion is the model comparison, not a latency-only proof.
