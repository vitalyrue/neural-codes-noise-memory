## What can be separated usefully

1. **Neural mechanism and model choices:** spikes renew decaying synaptic activation; weighted recurrent input sets rates; the circuit is tuned to a continuous family; stable disturbances return while a stored coordinate can remain displaced. Poisson spike generation, conditional independence, common synaptic time constant, optional symmetric weights, and the placement of noise are assumptions of the model. Keep these visible wherever a derived result is applied.
2. **General mathematical tools:** count variance and covariance; linearization; left/right eigenvectors and projection; diffusion and density evolution; local likelihood and Fisher information; Cauchy Schwarz and matrix inequalities; inference for a moving hidden value. These tools can be taught separately, but their formulas become the paper's neural predictions only after the model assumptions are supplied.
3. **Bridge and interpretation:** project this neural noise through this circuit's return flow to obtain D; derive J from the matching spike likelihood; compare D and J; then distinguish storage loss, observation error, and integrator leak. This bridge is essential. A physical-only account cannot fully explain why a disturbance changes the stored value without at least the geometry and projection idea.

The current reading path already supplies much of this separation: foundations, persistence geometry, neural dynamics, long-delay diffusion, information bound, readout. However, mathematical derivations remain inside the neural chapters, and the neural extensions are placed inside the readout chapter. A separate mathematics route or linked mathematical chapters could make that distinction easier to navigate. Simply moving all SI content to a mathematics appendix would also move the neural-model assumptions and interpretations there.
