Glossary

These short definitions support lookup after the explanations and examples in the main chapters.

  • abducens nucleus: A brainstem group containing the motor neurons recorded in the eye-drift study. Their axons activate the lateral rectus eye muscle.
  • activity bump: A localized peak of population firing rates across neurons ordered by their preferred represented values.
  • attractor manifold: The continuous family of persistent activity states, described by its represented coordinates rather than by every neural activity separately.
  • Betti numbers: Counts of independent connected components, loops, cavities, and their higher-dimensional counterparts.
  • Brownian motion: A continuous random process starting at zero with independent Gaussian increments of mean zero and variance equal to elapsed time.
  • causal filter: A rule whose current output depends only on present and past input.
  • coefficient of determination: For the optimal linear prediction with an intercept, the fraction of response variance removed by prediction. With one predictor it equals the squared correlation.
  • coefficient of variation: Standard deviation divided by mean. In the spike-train discussion it is applied to interspike intervals.
  • continuous attractor: A continuous family of fixed points with restoring dynamics toward the family and neutral motion along it in the ideal deterministic model.
  • convolution: An integral or sum combining past input values with a shifted response kernel to obtain a filtered output.
  • correlation coefficient: Covariance divided by the product of standard deviations, giving a dimensionless measure of linear association.
  • correlation length: The local distance scale \(\ell_c=\sqrt{-C(0)/C''(0)}\) for a smooth stationary spatial map. It compares input variation with variation of its slope; it need not specify covariance at large separations. Spatial-map explanation.
  • covariance: The mean product of two centered random variables, measuring their shared variation.
  • connection matrix: The matrix \(W\) of fixed connection weights. Entry \(W_{ij}\) maps sending neuron \(j\)’s synaptic activation into receiving neuron \(i\)’s input. Mechanism and example.
  • Cramér Rao bound: A lower bound on local unbiased estimation variance under the required regularity conditions, expressed through inverse Fisher information.
  • critical point: A point where a function’s gradient is zero. The Euler calculation also requires invertible Hessians.
  • decoding: Estimating a represented value from neural responses.
  • dendrite: A branching input structure of a neuron.
  • diffusion coefficient: The coefficient \(D\) in the local one-dimensional displacement variance \(2Dt\). Its units are coordinate squared per time.
  • diffusion tensor: The matrix giving local coordinate-displacement covariances per twice the elapsed time.
  • Dirac delta: An ideal unit impulse, with integral one across its location. In the spike equation it adds one unit of synaptic activation at the spike time. In the probability initial condition it concentrates all probability at one coordinate.
  • displacement: The change in a coordinate between two observations. For eye angle, \(\Delta x=x(t+\ell)-x(t)\) over lag \(\ell\).
  • elasticity: The tendency of stretched muscles and surrounding tissues to exert restoring forces.
  • encoding: Establishing a population activity pattern from an input.
  • estimator: A rule that converts observed data into an estimated value. The memory decoder estimates an angle or other represented coordinate from spikes.
  • Euler characteristic: The alternating sum of Betti numbers. In three dimensions, components minus independent loops plus enclosed cavities.
  • excess kurtosis: The fourth centered moment divided by the squared variance, minus three. It is zero for a Gaussian population.
  • excitatory input: In the models used here, an input that increases a receiving neuron’s tendency to fire.
  • excursion set: The positions where a function exceeds a specified level.
  • extraocular muscles: The six muscles attached to the outside of each eyeball that control its orientation.
  • Fano factor: Spike-count variance divided by mean count, for a specified counting interval and condition.
  • firing rate: The expected number of spikes per unit time, conditional on the modeled circumstances.
  • Fisher information: How sensitively the probabilities of observations change with a small change in the value being estimated. Formally, the expected squared score. In the independent Poisson model it compares squared changes in expected counts with count variance. Explanation and example; formal derivation.
  • fixational drift: Slow eye motion during attempted fixation, between rapid eye movements. The name includes random wandering; it does not imply systematic mean motion.
  • fixed point: A state whose deterministic rate of change is zero.
  • Fokker Planck equation: An equation describing how a probability density evolves under drift and diffusion. The convention is stated before using it.
  • gain: The scale that converts a change in one signal into a change in another.
  • gamma function: The function \(\Gamma(t)=\int_0^\infty u^{t-1}e^{-u}\,du\) for \(t>0\), extending factorials through \(\Gamma(n+1)=n!\).
  • Gaussian curvature: For a surface in three dimensions, the product of its two principal curvatures.
  • Gaussian process: A distribution over functions whose values at any finite collection of positions have a joint Gaussian distribution.
  • grid cell: A spatially tuned entorhinal neuron with repeated firing fields, often approximately hexagonally arranged in a two-dimensional arena.
  • Hessian: The matrix of second derivatives of a function with respect to its coordinates.
  • hippocampus: A brain region involved in memory and navigation.
  • hyperbolic cotangent: The function \(\coth z=1/\tanh z\), for real \(z\ne0\).
  • hyperbolic tangent: The function \(\tanh z=(e^z-e^{-z})/(e^z+e^{-z})\).
  • impulse response: The output of a linear filter in response to a unit-area input impulse, from which responses to general input can be assembled.
  • inertia: Resistance to a change in motion. Rotational inertia relates torque to angular acceleration; it does not supply a stationary holding torque.
  • inhibitory input: In the models used here, an input that reduces a receiving neuron’s tendency to fire.
  • internal Fisher information rate: The local information per unit observation time about the instantaneous attractor coordinate, as defined by the 2012 spike model.
  • inverse hyperbolic tangent: The inverse of \(\tanh\) on \((-1,1)\), given by \(\operatorname{artanh}z=\tfrac12\log[(1+z)/(1-z)]\).
  • instantaneous attractor state: The point on the attractor reached by following noise-free dynamics from a nearby network state. Its coordinate assigns a represented value even when the current activity lies slightly off the attractor. Explanation.
  • isotropy: Invariance of the spatial probability law under rotations.
  • Itô convention: A stochastic-increment convention that evaluates the noise coefficient at the beginning of each increment.
  • jointly Gaussian vector: A random vector for which every linear combination of its entries is Gaussian.
  • Kac–Rice formula: A way to compute expected crossing or critical-point counts from the joint distribution of a random function and its derivatives, under the relevant regularity assumptions.
  • left zero eigenvector: A vector \(v\) with \(v^T A=0\). With appropriate normalization it extracts the persistent coordinate from a small state disturbance.
  • likelihood: The probability or density of the observed data considered as a function of a proposed parameter value.
  • linearization: The first-order approximation to a dynamical equation near a reference state.
  • lognormal distribution: A distribution of positive values whose logarithms are Gaussian.
  • low pass filter: A filter that suppresses sufficiently rapid input changes relative to slower ones.
  • mean curvature: For a surface in three dimensions, the average of its two principal curvatures.
  • lag: The time separation between two observations, such as the start and end of a measured displacement.
  • line attractor: A one-dimensional continuous family of fixed points, with restoring dynamics toward the family and neutral motion along it in the ideal deterministic model. Explanation.
  • locally unbiased: An estimate whose mean equals the true value and has derivative one with respect to that value at the point being studied. Precision bound.
  • mean squared displacement: The average squared change in a coordinate across a specified time lag. An MSD curve plots this average against the lag; squared angle has units of degrees squared, while its square root has units of degrees.
  • median filter: A rule replacing each sampled value with a middle-ranked value from its local neighborhood. In the place maps it reduces isolated high or low rates and can change small field features.
  • microsaccades: Brief, rapid eye movements that occur during attempted fixation.
  • Morse theory: A theory relating the topology of a function’s level-set regions to its critical points. The analysis uses a signed critical-point count under its smoothness and nondegeneracy assumptions, including boundary contributions.
  • neural transfer function: The modeled relationship between a neuron’s input and its expected firing rate.
  • neuron: A signaling cell in the nervous system.
  • ocular motoneurons (OMNs): Neurons whose spikes activate the eye muscles.
  • ocular plant: The muscles and mechanical eye system that transform motor activity into eye motion.
  • oculomotor integrator: A neural circuit that converts movement commands into a maintained eye-position command.
  • one sided t test: A test of a mean against a reference value in a specified direction, using the mean difference divided by its estimated sampling uncertainty. The eye paper tests whether the mean cell correlation exceeds zero.
  • p value: Under the specified null comparison, the probability of a test statistic at least as extreme in the tested direction as the observed one.
  • path integration: Updating an estimate of position from movement.
  • place cell: A spatially selective hippocampal neuron that fires strongly in one or more localized regions of an environment.
  • place field: A connected region of elevated spatial firing, as defined by the model or stated measurement procedure.
  • plasticity: A lasting, experience-dependent change in neural responses or connections. In gaze control it can change how well the holding circuit is calibrated.
  • Poisson process: A counting process with independent increments and a deterministic constant or time-dependent event rate. Its count over an interval has equal mean and variance. In a recurrent model, conditionally Poisson instantaneous spike generation does not make complete spike trains have independent increments.
  • population code: A representation carried by the pattern of responses across neurons.
  • precision: The reciprocal of variance for a scalar Gaussian estimate.
  • preferred angle: An angle where a neuron’s tuning curve peaks.
  • prior distribution: A distribution representing knowledge about likely parameter values before observing the current data.
  • random field: A random function indexed by spatial position. For a Gaussian random field, values at every finite collection of positions are jointly Gaussian. One drawn map can remain fixed while an animal revisits positions. Meaning and model.
  • rate map: An estimate of spatial firing rate from spikes per time spent at each position.
  • Rayleigh distribution: The distribution of a positive variable that is a scaled square root of an exponential variable.
  • recurrent connectivity: Connections that feed activity back into the same neuronal population.
  • renewal process: An event process with independent, identically distributed intervals under the fixed conditions being modeled.
  • retina: The light-sensitive tissue at the back of the eye.
  • right zero eigenvector: A state-space direction \(u\) satisfying \(Au=0\) for the linearized dynamics.
  • root mean squared displacement: The square root of mean squared displacement, expressed in the original coordinate’s units.
  • saccade: A rapid eye movement that changes gaze direction.
  • score: The derivative of log likelihood with respect to the parameter being estimated.
  • skew: The third centered moment divided by the variance to the power \(3/2\), measuring asymmetry.
  • smoothness: The existence and regularity of derivatives of a function. The crossing and peak calculations require different derivative orders.
  • spatial covariance: The covariance of input values at two positions across possible spatial maps. For a stationary map it depends on separation, rather than absolute position. Spatial-map explanation.
  • Spearman correlation: Correlation computed after replacing observations by their ranks. It checks whether the two measured quantities tend to increase together without requiring a linear relation.
  • spike: A brief electrical event emitted by a neuron.
  • standard error of the mean (SEM): An estimate of uncertainty in a sample mean. Its interpretation depends on which observations are treated as independent, such as trials, cells, or animals.
  • state: The quantities that specify a modeled system’s current condition and determine its subsequent dynamics together with future inputs and noise.
  • Stationarity: In the place-field model, invariance of spatial statistics under translating the map.
  • stationary process: A random process whose probability law is unchanged by shifting its time or position origin. Weaker covariance-only stationarity must be specified when used.
  • Stratonovich convention: A stochastic-increment convention using midpoint evaluation in the limiting increments.
  • sub-Poisson spiking: Spiking with less variability than a matched Poisson reference according to the stated measure and conditions.
  • superdiffusion: Mean squared displacement growing faster than linearly with lag over the range being described.
  • synapse: A connection through which one neuron influences another.
  • synaptic activation: In the 2012 model, the decaying trace of a neuron’s outgoing spikes. Each spike adds one unit; connection weights convert this trace into input to other cells.
  • systematic drift: A nonzero mean velocity of the represented coordinate, distinct from random spreading.
  • tonic firing: Sustained neural firing during a holding period; the spike times need not be perfectly periodic.
  • torque: The turning effect of a force.
  • Translation invariance: In the readout model, invariance under shifts of the represented value. It makes the readout variance independent of the cue state.
  • translation symmetry: Shifting an activity pattern along the represented coordinate leaves the network dynamics unchanged in form. This describes the network dynamics. The readout calculation separately assumes that its estimation-error statistics are unchanged when the represented value is shifted.
  • tuning curve: A neuron’s expected firing rate as a function of a represented variable.
  • unbiased: Describes an estimator whose expected value equals the parameter locally under the conditions used in the estimation bound.
  • upcrossing: A point where a function crosses upward through a specified threshold.
  • viscosity: Mechanical resistance to motion that dissipates energy. It damps movement but supplies no holding torque at zero velocity.
  • weak fluctuation approximation: An expansion about the deterministic population dynamics in which the aggregate effects of many small spike contributions remain small.
  • Weibull distribution: A positive-variable distribution with survival probability \(\exp[-(s/b)^k]\), scale \(b>0\), and shape \(k>0\).
  • White noise: An ideal noise input with zero covariance at unequal times and a delta-function covariance at equal times. Its integrated increments have finite variance.

Physical implementation and neural memory

These terms are explained in the linked Part III passages.

  • analog state: A physical magnitude, such as capacitor voltage, used to represent a model quantity. Explanation.
  • bistability: Two locally stable states under the same ongoing conditions. Explanation.
  • device mismatch: Persistent differences between nominally identical circuit elements. Explanation.
  • digital storage: Discrete symbols represented by distinguishable physical ranges. Explanation.
  • in-memory computing: Performing operations where values are stored, reducing transfers to a separate arithmetic unit. Explanation.
  • mixed-signal hardware: Hardware combining analog circuit variables with digital storage, communication, or calculation. Explanation.
  • neuromorphic computing: Electronic computation inspired by neural organization or dynamics, with digital, analog, or combined implementations. Explanation.
  • nonvolatile storage: Storage that can retain its state without continuous power, within its retention limits. Explanation.
  • on-chip learning: Computing and applying some weight changes using circuitry on the chip. Explanation.
  • phase-change memory (PCM): Storage in a material atomic arrangement read through its resistance or conductance. Explanation.
  • pinning: Attraction of a nominally continuous memory toward preferred states. Explanation.
  • static random-access memory (SRAM): Digital storage whose powered transistor feedback maintains bits. Explanation.
  • working memory: Keeping information about recent input available for a later task. Explanation.
  • capacitor: A component that stores separated electric charge, with charge and voltage related by its capacitance. Explanation.
  • conductance: Current per unit applied voltage where current is approximately proportional to voltage. It is the inverse of resistance. Explanation.
  • current: Electric charge flowing per unit time. Explanation.
  • host computer: A separate computer running parts of an experiment outside the neuromorphic chip. Explanation.
  • register: A small, fast digital storage location. Explanation.
  • unwrapping: Tracking crossings of a full turn to continue an angular trajectory, when sampling resolves the crossings reliably. Explanation.
  • voltage: Electrical energy difference per unit charge. Explanation.

Simulation and methods

These terms describe how the models are executed and checked in Part IV.

  • Euler update: A numerical step that approximates change over a short interval by the current derivative times that interval.

  • fitted statistic: A measured summary used to choose a model’s parameters.

  • random seed: A value used to initialize a pseudorandom generator so that a specified implementation can repeat a sample sequence.

  • discretization: Representing a continuous variable such as time or position with finitely many sample points.

  • convergence check: A test of whether a reported quantity changes acceptably little when numerical resolution is refined.

  • Monte Carlo simulation: Estimating a quantity through repeated calculations with random samples.

  • Gaussian filter: A smoothing rule that averages nearby values with weights that decrease with distance according to a Gaussian function.
